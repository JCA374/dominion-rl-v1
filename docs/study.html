Study this project

For someone who wants to learn from this project rather than follow it: what to read in which order, the ideas you need, the words we use, how to run it yourself, and a few exercises.

Reading order

  1. Overview: the goal, the three methods and how "better" is measured. 5 minutes.
  2. How it sees: the 298 inputs and the 34 answers. 5 minutes.
  3. The model: imitation, then PPO with a league, and why this could beat a search. 15 minutes.
  4. Training and the Log: did it improve on its starting point (the genome) and where is it against the 70% line? 5 minutes.
  5. Lessons and the Podcast. 15 minutes.
  6. The code: c/rl.c (features, forward pass, sampling, recording: ~200 lines), rl/jobs.py (gae: the advantage in 12 lines), train.py (ppo_update: the PPO loss), experiments/goal_check.py. 1 hour.

Key concepts

Behaviour cloning

Supervised learning of an expert's choices: here, the GA genome's. Fast and stable, and it gives RL a strong start instead of a random player. Here: step 1 of train.py, the "bc_epoch" lines in the Log. What people get wrong: 98% agreement does not mean 98% of the strength: the 2% are often the hard decisions, and a copy can only match its teacher, never pass it.

Policy gradient

Change the network so that the choices in games that went well become more likely. The "how well" is the advantage. Here: ppo_update. What people get wrong: it does not know which of a game's 40 decisions won it; only averaging over many games sorts good decisions from lucky ones, which is why it needs millions of games.

Advantage and GAE

The advantage of a decision is how much better things went than the network itself expected at that moment. GAE builds it from the network's own win estimates along the game (λ = 0.95). Here: gae() in rl/jobs.py. What people get wrong: with a bad win estimate the advantages are bad too; the value head matters even though it never makes a decision.

PPO's clip

One update may change the probability of a recorded choice by at most ±20%; beyond that the gradient stops. It keeps learning from noisy data from destroying a good policy in one step. Where: clip, max_kl in CFG; the "PPO step size" chart on Training.

Exploration by sampling, evaluation by argmax

In training the network draws its choices at random from its probabilities; in every measurement it takes its top choice. What people get wrong: comparing the training games' win rate (with exploration) with the evaluation (without).

A league of opponents

Opponents from several sources so the policy stays good against all of them, not just the latest. Here: 40/15/15/30 GA, bots, past selves, itself. What people get wrong: pure self-play can cycle (A beats B beats C beats A) without getting better.

Reproduce it

8-core laptop CPU (i5-8250U), 8 GB RAM, Linux, Python 3.14, gcc:

git clone --recursive https://github.com/JCA374/dominion-rl-v1 && cd dominion-rl-v1
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu
.venv/bin/pip install numpy pytest pillow matplotlib
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests        # builds rl.so; "4 passed"
.venv/bin/python -u train.py --run mine     # imitation: ~1 min data + ~1 min per epoch; then PPO iterations
.venv/bin/python experiments/goal_check.py runs/mine/net_25.npy --name mine25 --vs_az   # ~4 min

Needs about 3 GB of free memory for the imitation data (1.08 million decisions × 298 inputs).

Exercises

  1. Easy: how good is a copy? Run the goal check on net_0.npy (imitation only). It agrees with the genome ~98% of the time; what does it score against the genome itself? Expect a bit under 50%.
  2. Medium: no league. Set vs_ga=0 vs_bots=0 vs_past=0 (pure self-play) and compare the evaluation against the GA after 50 iterations with the default run.
  3. Medium: exploration. ent=0 versus ent=0.03: watch the entropy chart and the score. Too little entropy: it stops trying things; too much: it plays randomly.
  4. Hard: which decisions does RL change? Compare net_0 and a late net on the GA genome's positions per decision kind (The model tab's "agreement" figure does it for the newest net).
