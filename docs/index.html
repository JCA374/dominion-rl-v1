One network that plays all of Dominion, learned by trial and error instead of search

The third method for the Dominion project. A single neural network makes every decision (what to buy, which action to play, what to trash, gain or discard), first by imitating the evolved GA genome, then improving by reinforcement learning (PPO, a policy-gradient method) against a league of opponents. No tree search at play time: one forward pass per decision. The goal: beat az-v1's best agent, which scores 70% against the GA base genome with 400 simulated futures per buy: above 70% on the same 1,000 games, and above 50% head to head against it.

What this is

Dominion is a deck-building card game: every turn you play action cards from your hand, then buy one card for your deck. There are about 20 kinds of decision: which action to play, what to buy, what Chapel trashes, what Workshop gains, what to discard when the opponent plays Militia, what Sentry does with the top cards… A game has about 40 real decisions per player (decisions with only one legal answer are not asked).

What came before. dominion-v1 evolved priority lists with a genetic algorithm (the "GA genome"): a fixed rule per kind of decision. az-v1 kept the genome's action play and replaced its buys with an AlphaZero-style search: 400 imagined futures per buy, guided by a network. That reached 70% against the GA genome and stopped there: 20 variants all landed between 66% and 70%.

What is different here. No search and no hand-written rule anywhere: the network answers every decision, including the action play that the other two methods never changed. It learns from the results of whole games (+1 win, −1 loss), the way a person improves by playing a lot: about a thousand games a second on this laptop.

The measure. az-v1's goal games exactly: 1,000 games against the GA base genome on 500 kingdoms never used in training, both seats (az-v1's best: 69.7% here). Then 1,000 head-to-head games against az-v1's best agent itself, on 500 other kingdoms. "Better" means both: above 70%, and above 50% head to head, with the 95% interval (±3 points) clear of the line.

Approach

1. Imitation. 20,000 games of the GA genome (against itself and the bots): 1.08 million decisions. The network learns to predict the genome's choice in each (and the winner), so it starts as a copy of the genome. 2. Reinforcement learning (PPO). Each iteration plays 3,000 games in which the network samples its decisions from its own probabilities, so it tries things. Every decision of a won game is made a little more likely, every decision of a lost one a little less, weighted by how much better or worse the game went than the network's own win estimate expected (the "advantage"). PPO limits how far one update may move the policy. 3. A league of opponents. 40% of the games against the GA genome, 15% against the three bots, 15% against its own past snapshots, 30% against itself. 4. Checks. After every update: 400 games against the GA genome and 300 against the bots with the network playing its top choice; every 25 iterations the goal check.

Real numbers (8-core laptop CPU, no GPU): the network has 151,331 weights (298 inputs → 256 → 256 → 34 choices + 1 win estimate) and runs in C inside the game engine: a whole game of network-vs-genome takes 3 ms, against 0.5 s for az-v1's searching agent. Training is PyTorch on the CPU.
