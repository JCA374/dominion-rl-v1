Lessons

What this project and earlier ones taught. Newest first.

From this project

  • Confirm once, on games never used, with the rival on the same games. The goal checks along r2 were selected by looking at them; the released net (r2 iteration 200) was then played once on 4,000 new games: 55.0% head to head against az-v1's best, and 70.3% vs the GA base where az-v1's best scored 69.0% on the very same games. Same games for both turns a 1-point difference from noise into a paired comparison.
  • A different method can beat a long-tuned one quickly. az-v1 needed a day of training and twenty variants to reach 70% and could not go further. Imitation + PPO + a league passed it in about three hours of training on the same laptop, because it changed what az-v1 could not (the action play) and learned from ten times more games.
  • You can only beat what you practise against. r1's league (GA genome, bots, past selves, itself) took the network to a tie with az-v1's best (49.2%, 49.4% head to head at iterations 100 and 200) and then nothing moved. Adding az-v1's agent to the league (15% its network without search, 5% with search) gave 54.3% and 55.4% head to head within 100 iterations. The score against the GA genome stayed at ~68%: beating one opponent is not the same as getting better in general.
  • Speed is a feature. The network plays a game in 3 ms inside the C engine; az-v1's search needs 460 ms. That is why this method got through ~0.9 million practice games in its first two hours, and why imitation + PPO overtook a search that took a day to train.
  • Imitation is a strong start, not a ceiling. The network copied the GA genome's choices 98.5% of the time and scored ~52% against it; PPO added 15 points in 100 iterations (25 minutes). Starting RL from a random network would have spent hours relearning what the genome already knew.

From az-v1 (that shaped this project)

  • Keep one number on data the model never trained on. az-v1's value head looked better and better in training (error 0.19) while it got worse on fresh games (1.17, worse than guessing 50/50). Here the win estimate is measured on each iteration's new games before training on them ("value error on fresh games").
  • A search is only as good as its judge. az-v1's searches with noisy judgements found bad moves that looked good by luck; with half of its judge being the GA genome playing on, it could not get past ~70%. This project has no judge at play time: it learns from real game results.
  • When the student agrees with the teacher on everything, the teaching has stopped. az-v1's net copied its search 98% of the time and the loop went flat for 250 iterations. Policy gradients don't have a teacher to agree with: the games themselves are the signal.
