From az-v1 (that shaped this project)

  • Keep one number on data the model never trained on. az-v1's value head looked better and better in training (error 0.19) while it got worse on fresh games (1.17, worse than guessing 50/50). Here the win estimate is measured on each iteration's new games before training on them ("value error on fresh games").
  • A search is only as good as its judge. az-v1's searches with noisy judgements found bad moves that looked good by luck; with half of its judge being the GA genome playing on, it could not get past ~70%. This project has no judge at play time: it learns from real game results.
  • When the student agrees with the teacher on everything, the teaching has stopped. az-v1's net copied its search 98% of the time and the loop went flat for 250 iterations. Policy gradients don't have a teacher to agree with: the games themselves are the signal.
