The model

One network, three uses: it decides, it estimates its chance to win, and (in imitation) it copies.

The network

 298 inputs (what I know + the question)
   │  dense 298 → 256, ReLU
   │  dense 256 → 256, ReLU
   ├──▶ 34 logits: a card id (or a Sentry option) or PASS; illegal answers masked out → softmax → a choice
   └──▶ 1 number: tanh → my win estimate in [−1, +1]
 151,331 weights; one forward pass ≈ 20 µs in C

It is the same small multilayer perceptron for every kind of decision: the "question" inputs tell it what is being asked. Playing to win, it takes the highest-scoring legal answer; in training it samples one in proportion to its probabilities, which is how it explores.

Step 1: imitation (behaviour cloning)

1.08 million decisions of the GA genome (20,000 games against itself and the bots). Loss = cross-entropy between the network's probabilities and the genome's choice, plus half the squared error of the win estimate against the game's result. After it, the network makes the genome's choice about 97% of the time, and plays roughly as well as the genome: the starting point of RL is "the GA genome", not a random player. A random network loses every game.

Step 2: reinforcement learning (PPO)

Each iteration: 3,000 games with the network sampling. For each of its decisions the game records what it saw, what it chose, how likely that choice was, and its win estimate at that moment. At the end the game gives +1 (win), −1 (loss) or 0 (shared).

Advantage. Was a decision better or worse than expected? GAE (generalised advantage estimation) compares the win estimate after the decision with the one before it, chained back from the final result: a decision after which the estimate rose gets a positive advantage. λ = 0.95 mixes short-term (next estimate) and long-term (final result).

Update. Make decisions with positive advantage more likely and negative ones less likely, but by at most ±20% in probability ratio per update (the PPO clip), 3 passes over the data, and stop early if the policy moved too far (KL > 0.03). The win estimate learns the observed returns. A small entropy bonus keeps it from becoming certain too early.

The league. Learning only against itself, a policy can drift into strategies that beat itself and lose to others; learning only against the GA genome, it overfits that one opponent. So: 40% GA genome, 15% bots, 15% past snapshots (every 10th iteration, the last 8 kept), 30% itself (both seats learning).

Why this might beat a search

az-v1's search found better buys than the genome, but every non-buy decision stayed the genome's, and its judge of a position was half a genome rollout, so it could not get past what the genome's own play makes possible. This network changes everything at once and learns from millions of games instead of hundreds of simulations. The risks are the classic ones of policy gradients: very noisy learning signal (one result per ~40 decisions), forgetting the good imitation, and collapsing onto a strategy that only beats its own league.
