# Episode 02: Beating the search at its own game

Last time, the new player had caught up with the old one. One network, no search, making every decision itself, after about half an hour of practice. Against the evolved list it won sixty seven percent. Head to head against the searching player, it won forty nine percent of a thousand games. That is a tie.

Then it stopped improving. For another hundred rounds the numbers hardly moved. Sixty eight percent against the evolved list. Forty nine against the searcher. The practice games were the problem. It played the evolved list, three simple bots, its own older versions, and itself. Never the searching player. So it never learned what beats that one.

So I added it to the practice. The searching player is slow, half a second per game, because it imagines four hundred futures for every buy. So only one practice game in twenty is against the full searcher. Another three in twenty are against its network alone, without the search, which is almost free. That network buys like the searcher, minus the careful checking.

The effect was fast. Fifty rounds later, about twenty five minutes, the new player won fifty four percent of a thousand fresh games against the searcher. After a hundred rounds, fifty five and a half. Those games were on card sets it had never practised on, and the range of likely values is clear of fifty. For the first time, the network is better than the searcher in a direct match.

Why can a player that never looks ahead beat one that imagines four hundred futures? I think there are three reasons. First, it plays its own action cards. The searcher still uses the evolved list for every action, discard and trash. The new player learned its own way. Second, it has practised nearly a million games. The searcher's network learned from about three hundred and fifty thousand. Third, it practised against the searcher, so it could find exactly where the searcher's habits are weak.

That third reason is also a warning. Learning to beat one opponent is not the same as getting better in general. And there is a sign of that. Against the evolved list it still wins only sixty eight percent. The searcher wins seventy against the same list. The difference is within the noise of a thousand games, but it is not the clear win I want.

So the goal is half met. Better in a direct match, yes. Better against the common opponent, not yet. Training continues, and I am watching both numbers.
